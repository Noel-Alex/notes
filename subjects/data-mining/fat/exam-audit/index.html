04 / 2025 real-paper preparation

Four fully worked FAT questions

Paraphrased prompts based on the readable 2025 F1 paper. Calculations are independently worked and explanatory; they are not a reproduced official answer key. Try each on paper before revealing.

Q1 / 10 marks / Module 2

Design mining functionalities for a retail business

Prompt: A consumer-electronics retailer has customer, sales and product records. Which data-mining functionalities could turn those data into business insight? Justify each with a real example.

Reveal complete answer plan and examples
  1. Characterization: Summarize the typical high-value customer, e.g. frequent buyers have a higher average order amount.
  2. Discrimination: Compare high-spending and low-spending groups on purchases and shopping channels.
  3. Frequent patterns / association: Find bundles: laptop ⇒ mouse, with transaction support and rule confidence.
  4. Classification: Learn from labeled customers to predict whether a new customer will churn (yes/no).
  5. Prediction / regression: Predict next month's sales revenue as a numeric quantity.
  6. Clustering: Segment customers into behavior groups without pre-assigned labels.
  7. Outlier detection: Flag unusually large orders or refund patterns for further investigation.
  8. Evolution / trend mining: Identify seasonal accessory demand and shifts over time.

Exam close: Explain which data, output and business decision go with each method. Never interchange classification, clustering and numeric prediction.

Q2 / 10 marks / historical-only extension

Chi-square independence test — 2 × 3 table

Prompt: For males and females choosing between three product categories, test whether category preference and gender are independent at the 5% significance level.

Reveal calculation, degrees of freedom and inference
GenderCategory ACategory BCategory CTotal
Male305020100
Female206020100
Total5011040200
  1. Set H₀: gender and preference are independent. H₁: they are associated.
  2. Calculate each expected frequency as (row total × column total)/grand total. For both rows: E = (25, 55, 20).
  3. Compute Σ (Observed − Expected)² / Expected over all six cells.
χ² = 25/25 + 25/55 + 0/20 + 25/25 + 25/55 + 0/20 = 1 + 0.4545 + 1 + 0.4545 = 2.9091 Degrees of freedom = (2 − 1)(3 − 1) = 2 Critical χ²(α=0.05, df=2) ≈ 5.991

Decision: 2.9091 < 5.991 ⇒ fail to reject H₀ at 5%. The data do not provide sufficient evidence of association. This does not prove independence.

Portion warning: This calculation was on the historical 2025 paper, but chi-square is not a named heading in your current uploaded BCSE208L outline.

Q3 / 10 marks / Module 4

Apriori — candidates, pruning and strong rules

Prompt: Given five market-basket transactions, mine all itemsets with minimum 60% support and generate rules with confidence at least 80%.

Reveal all scans, frequent itemsets and qualifying rules

Transactions (letters denote items): T100={M,O,N,K,E,Y}; T200={D,O,N,K,E,Y}; T300={M,A,K,E}; T400={M,U,C,K,Y}; T500={C,O,O,K,I,E}. Notice that T500 contains O twice: within one transaction, an item is only counted once for itemset support.

Minimum support count = ceil(0.60 × 5) = 3; minimum rule confidence = 80%.

PassSurviving frequent itemsets (count)Reason
L1E:4, K:5, M:3, O:3, Y:3Count each distinct transaction containing item
L2EK:4, EO:3, KM:3, KO:3, KY:3Count candidates; discard support below 3
L3EKO:3All of its two-item subsets must occur in L2
L4NoneNo further candidate survives

Strong rules with confidence ≥80%:

E ⇒ K: 4 / 4 = 100% K ⇒ E: 4 / 5 = 80% O ⇒ E: 3 / 3 = 100% M ⇒ K: 3 / 3 = 100% O ⇒ K: 3 / 3 = 100% Y ⇒ K: 3 / 3 = 100% O ⇒ EK: 3 / 3 = 100% EO ⇒ K: 3 / 3 = 100% KO ⇒ E: 3 / 3 = 100%

Why no others? E⇒O has 3/4=75%; E⇒KO likewise 3/4=75%; K⇒O=3/5=60%; EK⇒O=3/4=75%. Always compute confidence of each candidate rule, not just support.

Q4 / 10 marks / Module 4

FP-Growth — filter, sort and construct the FP-tree

Prompt: With a long transaction record containing infrequent items and some repeated labels, build the FP-tree and identify the final frequent patterns at minimum support count 3.

Reveal all transaction filtering and the final tree

Transactions: T1={A,B,C,E,F}; T2={G,E,F}; T3={A,H,I,J}; T4={A,I,J,K,L,M,N,O,P,Q,R,S,T,U,V,W,X,Y,Z}; T5={J,B,B,D,K,E}.

  1. Count transaction presence, not repeated occurrences within a transaction: A=3, E=3, J=3. B=2 and F=2; remaining items are less than 3.
  2. Discard all infrequent items. Choose a consistent global descending support order, resolving the A/E/J tie alphabetically.
  3. Filtered transactions: T1=A→E; T2=E; T3=A→J; T4=A→J; T5=E→J.
  4. Insert paths incrementally, increasing existing prefix-node counts rather than creating duplicate prefix nodes.
ROOT ├── A : 3 │ ├── E : 1 │ └── J : 2 └── E : 2 └── J : 1

Frequent singletons: {A}:3, {E}:3, {J}:3. No frequent pair reaches support count 3: AE=1, AJ=2, EJ=1. Therefore no frequent triplet exists.

Exam trap: A long transaction does not make all its items frequent. Keep a consistent global order, ignore duplicate occurrences inside a single transaction, and check threshold counts.
